How to use SiliconAperture
  1. Drop a photo onto the left panel.
  2. If the image contains EXIF metadata, camera model, sensor and focal length are detected automatically. Otherwise select your camera from the dropdown or enter sensor dimensions and focal length manually.
  3. Click two points on the photo to mark the object's extent. The pixel span fills in.
  4. Enter the distance to the object in meters, or click two points on the satellite map to measure it.
  5. The real-world size appears as soon as all four inputs are valid.

Flip the Solve for toggle to Distance to work it the other way: enter an object's known real-world size and the tool returns the distance to it, then click the object on the satellite map to draw a ring of possible camera positions.

Solve for distance
D = size · f / √( (Δx·p)² + (Δy·p)² )

The pixel size comes from the sensor size and the photo's pixel count. The sensor's long side lies along the photo's long side, so portrait photos and crops (16:9, 1:1) work too. When the photo's EXIF gives a 35 mm equivalent that does not fit the camera database's main camera (a telephoto or ultrawide lens, or a digital zoom), the sensor size is taken from the EXIF instead. Works for marks at any angle.

Accurate results require the object to be perpendicular to the camera axis. If the surface is angled, the apparent extent seen by the lens no longer maps linearly to the true physical size, and the result will be inaccurate. D is the distance along the camera's axis: a distance measured on the map is along the ground, which is close to it for an object near the middle of the photo and at about the camera's height.

A photo with GPS in its EXIF flies the map there, so the map tiles for that place are fetched from the tile server.
